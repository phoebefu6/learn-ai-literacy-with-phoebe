# Presenter notes - Session 1 · What AI actually is

## Preflight (5 min before)
- Open courses/01-what-ai-is.html, Projector zoom 125%.
- Test the sorter widget once (one right, one wrong click).
- Have one live "AI-powered" claim ready to dissect (a vendor page or headline from this week).
- If the room did the data-literacy course, name that this is the sequel - the wall it stopped at.

## Run of show
- 0-3 · Welcome. Ask: "how many different things has someone called AI this week?" Set the promise: by the end, no vendor pitch confuses you.
- 3-8 · Part 1 nested dolls. Draw the four rings on the whiteboard even with the SVG up. Drill the which-doll test. This is the session's spine.
- 8-13 · Part 2 three eras. The two winters land the humility. Hit "idea or scale?" hard - it is the sharpest question they leave with.
- 13-18 · Part 3 right-tool test. This is the money slide. Say it plainly: the best AI skill is knowing when not to use it. Rogati hierarchy callback.
- 18-26 · Apply-along 1 sorter. Everyone sorts. Items "flag over $10k" and "shipping cost" (the two non-AI ones) cause the best debate - let it run.
- 26-33 · Apply-along 2 audit a claim. Circulate. Push them to name the doll and "idea or scale?".
- 33-40 · Apply-along 3 their own backlog. Quiet, personal. Harvest 2-3 "this is really a data problem" realizations.
- 40-45 · Q&A + quiz.

## Never cut
- The which-doll test (Part 1) - the whole course hangs off it.
- The right-tool test (Part 3) - the highest-ROI idea in the course.
- Apply-along 1's two non-AI items - the point that "should we?" precedes "can we?".

## Cut if long
- Part 2 self-study AGI card - point at it.
- Apply-along 2 - compress to one claim dissected from the front.
- The cost-of-over-using-AI card - self-study pointer.

## Expected questions
- "Is AGI coming / will it take my job?" - Every deployed AI is narrow. AGI is contested and does not exist today. Plan for narrow tools with clear metrics.
- "So is generative AI overhyped?" - The tech is real; the word "AI" slapped on everything is the hype. Name the doll and judge case by case.
- "We tried AI and it failed." - Usually a rule's job given to a model, on data that was not clean. The right-tool test + Hierarchy of Needs prevent it.
- "Do we need our own AI team?" - Session 8 (build/buy/API). Usually API-first, build only your true differentiator.
- "Where does the data-literacy course fit?" - Underneath all of this. Clean, reliable data is the floor every AI stands on.
